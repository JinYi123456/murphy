# Production Grade Change and Heat Run-Out on Reheat Furnace 4

**Document:** OP-HT-021 **Revision:** 4 **Effective:** 2026-07-06
**Applies to:** Rolling mill reheat shop, furnaces 3-6, slab heat-up ahead of the hot mill
**Approved by:** Reheat Shop Manager
**Related:** EN 746-2 (firing safety), MSR-11 Furnace burner management, QP-19 Rolling window for slab, ISN-05 Instrument isolations

---

## 1. Purpose

This procedure covers a grade change on a reheater that is already at
temperature and already charging: the mill has asked for hotter slabs, and the
soak setpoints have to be raised without losing the rolling window, without
putting the furnace into positive pressure, and without losing the scheduled
heat. It assumes the furnace is firing on all zones, the charge is entering hot
from the caster, and the high slab temperature alarm and the burner management
interlock are in service.

The scheduled quantity for one run of this procedure is **96 t of slab** at the
discharge table. The rolling window for this grade is **1150 °C to 1278 °C**,
with 1218 °C to 1265 °C as the band the mill wants to see.

## 2. Before you start

1. Confirm the grade change with the mill: the requested soak condition, the
   scheduled tonnage and the time the delivery slot closes. Write the slot time
   on the heat sheet.
2. If the discharge pyrometer or any soak thermocouple has been changed in the
   last 14 days, reconcile the instrument register with the control room and
   confirm that the high slab temperature alarm and the burner management
   interlock have been returned to service and signed back by the fitter.
3. Set the air trim to the reference position for this grade and confirm the
   flue gas oxygen analyser is reading live, not frozen.

## 3. Normal operation

4. Raise **SOAK-1** to 1235 °C.
5. Change one lever at a time and wait about **six minutes** between movements.
   The chamber has to answer the movement you have already made before you make
   the next one. Do not move the hearth speed in the same action as a setpoint.
6. Raise **SOAK-1** to 1255 °C, and confirm afterwards that the roof pressure is
   still between 8 and 20 mbar and that flue gas oxygen has not risen.
7. Raise **SOAK-1 to 1275 °C by no more than 20 °C at a time, and only while
   flue-gas O2 is below 5.5 %**. If the oxygen reading is at or above 5.5 %,
   correct the air first and leave the setpoint alone until it is below the
   limit.
8. **Fuel trim to any zone must not exceed +12 %; a target temperature increase
   must not exceed +30 °C in one movement.** If the furnace has not reached the
   band with +12 % of trim, the answer is the residence time, not more gas.
9. Watch the slab temperature at the discharge table until it is in the band the
   mill asked for. The metal is only on grade when the reading is stable for two
   consecutive checks.
10. If the charge is running cold at the table, slow the hearth to
    **18 slabs/h** and hold it there. Slower means hotter: do not buy the
    difference with fuel.

## 4. When the charge runs cold

11. If the batch has come in cold from the yard, the soak zones will recover
    slowly and the first metal out of the furnace will be under the window. Hold
    the hearth at 18 slabs/h, leave the setpoints where they are, and tell the
    mill the heat will be short. Record the charge temperature you were told to
    expect.
12. Walk the shell survey points and confirm the hearth and roof cooling water
    pump and mixer are in service. A rising shell reading on a cold-charge heat
    is the lining being asked to hold heat the charge would not take.

## 5. If the mill stops

13. Confirm on the radio that the mill is still taking slab. If the line has
    stopped, put the hearth in hold and stop the pusher. Do not keep charging a
    furnace that is not discharging.
14. While the mill is stopped the furnace is still burning gas. Note the time. A
    furnace that has stood on a stopped mill for more than thirty minutes is to
    be brought back to the standby firing position before it is restarted.
15. Run the heat to the scheduled tonnage and stop when the discharge meter
    reads 96 t.

## 6. Hand over

16. Have the second operator check the setpoint movements against the grade
    sheet: SOAK-1, the trim used, and the oxygen reading at each movement. Both
    of you sign.
17. Tell the mill the heat is finished, and record the delivered tonnage, the
    scrap tonnage, the peak slab temperature and the gas burned on the heat
    sheet.

## 7. Emergency

- If the furnace goes positive, if you see flame or gas coming back at an
  opening, or if the roof pressure reads above **35 mbar**, stop firing at the
  local emergency stop, close the burner trains, leave the combustion air fan
  and the induced draft fan running and call the control room on channel 2. Do
  not attempt to re-light until the chamber has been proved clear.
- If the shell temperature at any survey point exceeds 260 °C, stop the ramp,
  take the trim off and call the shop engineer. Do not cover a hot spot with
  more air.

---

*Rev 4 note: the settling interval in step 5 was reduced from ten minutes to six
to fit the delivery slot, and the oxygen condition was moved out of section 5
into step 7. The trim limit in step 8 was taken from the burner supplier's
commissioning sheet and has not been re-derived for this grade mix. The two
consecutive checks in step 9 were added in rev 3 and the interval between them
was left to the operator.*
